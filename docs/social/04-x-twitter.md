## 1. WHAT X IS HONESTLY FOR

**X will not get you streams.** Say it once, believe it, and then the platform becomes useful.

What X actually does for an independent artist:

1. **It's where you meet people.** Music journalists, playlist curators, small-label people, Substack writers, and — most valuable of all — other independent artists at exactly your level. Instagram has no mechanism for this. X does, and it's the reply.
2. **It's the cheapest place to be a *writer*.** You have ~100 diary entries and a written voice. There is no other platform where prose is a first-class object.
3. **It's the permanent-link layer.** A blog, a Substack, a curator — when they want to look you up and see whether you're a person, they check X. It's the reference surface.
4. **It's the lowest-effort platform in the campaign.** Text costs you nothing to produce and X tolerates volume.

**The correct expectation:** if X delivers 200 streams across the entire campaign, that's normal. If it delivers one curator relationship, one blog write-up and three artist friendships, it paid for itself ten times over. **Measure X in relationships, not numbers.**

# 3. TRACK A — THE PROMOTIONAL LAYER

## 3.1 The register

X promo reads as **dry, flat, and slightly self-deprecating.** The Instagram caption "I've been sitting on this for eight months and it's the truest thing I've made 🖤" is correct for Instagram and wrong here. On X the same thought is:

```
song's out friday. i've had it finished since february and have been too scared to press the button.
```

Rules:
- Lowercase, mostly
- No emoji, or one, rarely
- **Never use Instagram-native language** — "link in bio", "swipe up", "🖤"
- Don't thank people for engagement
- Don't post "thoughts?" under your own content
- **The post that does best on X is the one that says something true that nobody else is saying**

## 3.4 The networking protocol — the actual point of X

This is worth more than every promo post above combined, and it takes twenty minutes a day.

**Follow ~150 accounts, in these proportions:**

| Who | How many | How to find them |
|---|---|---|
| Independent artists at your level (under ~5k followers) | 60 | Search `#newmusic`, reply guys under bigger artists, people posting their own release days |
| Independent artists one level up (5k–50k) | 20 | Artists whose "Discovered On" playlists you're targeting |
| Playlist curators | 20 | Their X is usually linked from their Spotify profile |
| Music journalists and bloggers | 20 | Bylines from Rolling Stone India, Homegrown, Wild City, Substack music writers |
| Small labels and A&R | 10 | Labels that put out artists like you |
| Producers / engineers | 10 | For the craft conversations |
| Indian independent scene accounts | 10 | The scene is small and it's on X |

**The daily 20 minutes:**
- **Reply to 10 posts. Zero of them about yourself.** Reply with something useful, funny, or specific. Not "🔥". Not "this!". An actual sentence.
- **Quote-post one thing you genuinely like**, with a real thought attached
- **Listen to one song someone posted, and say something specific about it.** Not "great track" — "the way the drums drop out at 1:40 is the whole song." People remember specificity and they will listen to yours.
- **Never DM a cold pitch.** Ever. Build the relationship in public for six weeks first.

**The rule that makes this work:** for the first 30 days, your reply-to-own-post ratio should be at least **10:1**. If someone's first exposure to you is a reply that says something smart about their song, they'll check your profile. If it's a link to yours, they won't.

⭐ **The highest-leverage single post on X for a new artist:**
```
what's a song you love that has under 100k streams? genuinely collecting.
```
Post it at T-40 and again at T+30. Reply to every single answer, and listen to them. You will meet more artists in those replies than in a year of DMs, and every one of them is a person who releases music and might mention yours.

# 4. TRACK B — THE DIARY

## 4.1 The structural decision (read this before you post entry one)

You want a single thread of ~100 entries. Here's the problem with that, and what I'd do instead.

**What a true 100-post self-reply thread gets you:**
- One object. One link. Bookmarkable, shareable as a whole.
- The serial feeling — people follow *to keep reading*
- New entries appear in your followers' timelines

**What it costs you, and this is the part that matters:**
- **Self-replies get meaningfully less reach than standalone posts on X.** Replies are deprioritised in the For You feed. Entry 60 of a thread reaches a fraction of what entry 60 would reach as its own post. Across 100 entries, that's the difference between the diary being read and the diary being a private archive with a public URL.
- **X collapses long threads.** A reader arriving at post 40 sees "Show this thread" and, realistically, doesn't.
- **There's no entry point.** A new follower at day 60 cannot start at entry 1, because entry 1 is buried under 59 replies.

### ⭐ The recommendation: the numbered series with a spine

Three components. It gets you the serial feeling *and* full reach.

**1. Every entry is a standalone post.** Not a reply. Full algorithmic treatment.

**2. Every entry is numbered.** `[i.]`, `[xlvii.]`, `41/`, or just a bare number — pick one mark and never change it. The number is what turns 100 separate posts into one object in the reader's head. It's the cheapest serialisation device there is and it costs you three characters.

**3. A pinned spine post**, updated as you go:
```
i'm writing 100 of these.

about one person. not about the song, though the song came out of them.

they're all here, in order: [link to a chapter thread / your site]

no. 41 as of today.
```

**4. Chapter threads.** Every ten entries, post a real thread that collects the last ten, in order, as quote-posts or retyped. Ten chapter threads across the campaign. Each one is:
   - an entry point for new readers ("start at chapter 1")
   - a fresh full-reach post about content you already made
   - a natural rhythm marker — it makes the thing feel structured rather than endless

**5. If you have the website** (the master plan mentions one): mirror all 100 there, in order, on one page. That's the canonical object. X is the distribution; the site is the archive. This solves every entry-point problem permanently and it's an afternoon of work.

> If you want the pure single-thread version anyway — because the *form* matters to you more than the reach — it's a legitimate artistic choice and I'd respect it. Just make it deliberately, knowing it costs you roughly two-thirds of the readership.

## 4.2 The rules of the diary

Non-negotiables. Break these and it stops working.

1. **No names.** Not hers, not anyone's. The moment a name appears it becomes about a person instead of about the feeling, and every reader loses their way in.
2. **Second person or third person, never both in one entry.** Pick per entry. "you" is more intimate and more dangerous. Use it for maybe 25 of the 100.
3. **No metaphor stacking.** One image per entry, maximum. The power in this form is plainness. `the door was open` beats `the door was open like a question nobody asked`.
4. **Specificity over scale.** A bus number, a brand of biscuit, the exact hour. Never "everything", "always", "forever".
5. **Don't explain the song.** The diary and the song are siblings, not a song and its commentary. If an entry ends with "and that's what the second verse is about", cut that line.
6. **Don't resolve it.** No entry should read like a conclusion. Not even entry 100. Especially not entry 100.
7. **Never make her a character.** She doesn't speak, doesn't act, isn't described. She's the shape of an absence. This is also the only ethical way to write publicly about a real person.
8. **Six of these should be funny.** Genuinely funny. A hundred entries of unbroken sincerity is unreadable, and the funny ones are what make the sad ones land.
9. **Write out of order.** If they run chronologically, readers start waiting for the ending instead of reading each one.
10. **One a day, no more.** Two in a day breaks the rhythm and the form depends on the rhythm.

## 4.3 The length rhythm

A hundred entries of identical length is a wall. Vary them deliberately, to this distribution:

| Length | Count | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **Fragment** — under 12 words | **32** | A jolt. Often the best ones. |
| **Short** — 1–2 sentences | **34** | The baseline |
| **Medium** — 3–6 sentences | **21** | A small scene |
| **Long** — a paragraph | **10** | Needs X Premium or a screenshot |
| **Very long** — multiple paragraphs | **3** | Save for entry ~50, ~85, and one more |

**The sequencing rules:**
- **Never two long entries in a row.** After a long one, the next is a fragment. Always.
- **Open with a fragment.** Entry 1 should be under 12 words. It teaches the reader the form in four seconds and it sets the expectation that this won't be a chore.
- **Every fifth entry should be a fragment**, roughly, as a breath.
- **The three very-long entries are events.** Space them at least 25 entries apart.

## 4.7 The mechanics

- **One entry per day, posted at the same time.** Consistency is the form. I'd use **10:30pm IST** — late enough to feel like a diary, and it catches the UK evening.
- **Numbering:** pick one system and never change it. Roman numerals look better and are harder to type. Arabic is more legible on mobile. `41/100` telegraphs the ending and makes people wait for it — **I'd avoid the /100**.
- **Line breaks matter enormously.** A single line with white space around it hits ten times harder than the same line in a paragraph. Use X's line breaks generously.
- **Long entries:** if you don't have Premium, post them as a **screenshot of plain text** — white on black, one font, generous margins. This is a well-established form and it reads beautifully. Always add the text as alt text so it stays searchable and accessible.
- **Never delete an entry.** If you change your mind, write a correction. That's what entry type 6 is for, and the corrections are some of the strongest entries in the whole form.
- **Don't reply to people inside the diary's replies with promo.** Someone says "this is beautiful" — say thank you. Don't say "the song's out friday". That trade kills the thing.
- **The chapter threads** (§4.1) go up on the day after every tenth entry.

# 5. HOW THE TWO TRACKS COEXIST

This is the real risk in the plan: **the diary makes the promo posts feel cheap, and the promo posts make the diary feel like marketing.** Both directions are fatal.

## 5.1 The separation rules

1. **Never in the same hour.** Diary at 10:30pm. Promo in the morning or early evening. A hard gap of at least six hours.
2. **Never in the same post.** No entry ever ends with a link. No promo post is ever written in the diary's voice.
3. **The diary never sells.** Not once. Not "the song this is all about is out friday". The diary's entire power is that it isn't asking for anything, and one ask contaminates all hundred entries retroactively.
4. **The promo may reference the diary — once, obliquely.** In the release thread (§3.3, post 4): *"i've written about most of it in the other thread on this account."* That's it. That's the only bridge. It works because it's an offer, not a pitch.
5. **On release day, the diary still posts its entry, and the entry is not about the release.** This is the single strongest move available to you on this platform. Everyone else's account becomes an advert for one day. Yours posts entry 46 as if nothing is happening. People will notice and it will say more about you than the thread does.

## 5.2 Which entries land where

| Day | Diary entry | Why |
|---|---|---|
| T-30 (announcement) | An ordinary fragment | Contrast |
| **T (release day)** | **An ordinary entry, unrelated** | ⭐ See above |
