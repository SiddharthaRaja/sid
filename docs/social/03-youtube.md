## 0. THE CONSTRAINT, STATED PLAINLY

You've said: **one music video, and probably nothing else filmed.** Shorts will mirror Instagram Reels.

That's a workable plan, but it has one specific hole, and it's worth naming before anything else:

> **One video is one upload. A YouTube channel with one upload is not a channel.**

YouTube is the only platform in this campaign where the *long-form* surface is the thing with staying power — a music video keeps getting found for years, a Reel does not. But YouTube's system needs something to recommend, and a channel with a single video gives it nothing to work with. The good news: **a long-form YouTube upload does not have to be produced.** Six of the eight uploads below need no camera crew, no location, and in most cases no camera at all.

**The slate this document builds, from what you already have:**

| # | Upload | What it actually requires | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Official music video | The shoot you're already doing | **T** |
| 2 | Official audio / visualiser | One static image or a 6s loop + the audio. 20 minutes in any editor. | **T** |
| 3 | Lyric video | Already being made | **T+14** |
| 4 | "Director's commentary" — the song playing with you talking over it | A microphone. That's it. | T+21 |
| 5 | Making-of | **Conditional** — only exists if someone films BTS on a phone on shoot day | T+28 |
| 6 | Acoustic one-take | One camera, one mic, your desk, 12 minutes | **T+42** |
| 7 | "Everything I learned releasing my first single" | You, a phone, 10 minutes | **T+50** |
| 8 | Extended / slowed / sped versions | Rendered from the master, zero filming | rolling |

**Eight uploads across 100 days. One of them is a production. That's the whole point of this document.**

And the one thing worth reconsidering, again: **ask someone to film BTS on a phone all day at the video shoot.** It costs nothing, needs no extra day, and it's the difference between 7 uploads and 8 — plus ~15 Shorts in a visual world that isn't the polished video.

## 1. WHAT YOUTUBE IS ACTUALLY FOR

Three things, and only one of them is what people expect.

**1. It's where the money is — but not from ads.** For a musician, YouTube revenue comes overwhelmingly from **Content ID**, not the Partner Programme. Content ID claims every piece of user-generated content that contains your song and pays you for it, and it has **no subscriber threshold at all**. You get it through DistroKid's opt-in. The Partner Programme (ads on your own videos) needs 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours, which you almost certainly will not hit on a debut single — and that's fine, because it's the smaller number anyway.

> ⚠️ **One date worth knowing:** YouTube is doubling the Partner Programme entry bar on **1 February 2027** — to 8,000 watch hours (from 4,000) or 20 million Shorts views (from 10 million). Subscribers stay at 1,000. If your release lands well before that and a long-form video takes off, getting in under the old bar is worth a small amount of deliberate effort. If not, ignore it — Content ID is the real money and it's unaffected.

**2. It's the permanent record.** A Reel is dead in 48 hours. A music video on YouTube gets found in year three by someone searching your name. Every other platform in this campaign is renting attention; YouTube is the only one where you own an asset that appreciates.

**3. Shorts is the best free reach on the internet right now.** Over 200 billion daily views, and a much less saturated music-creator pool than TikTok. **You are already making the files.** Posting them to Shorts is a copy-paste with a different title. This is the highest effort-to-reward ratio in the entire campaign and it takes four minutes a day.

**What YouTube is not:** a place where you post daily, a place where you chase subscribers, or a place where a 20-minute vlog makes sense for you. Eight good uploads beats forty bad ones, permanently.

## 3. THE EIGHT LONG-FORM UPLOADS

### ▸ UPLOAD 1 — THE OFFICIAL MUSIC VIDEO · T

**Premiere it.** Schedule a Premiere rather than a plain upload: it creates a countdown page you can share for days beforehand, viewers can set reminders, there's a live chat, and the concentrated first-hour viewership is a real ranking signal.

- Set the Premiere for **T, at the time the song goes live on DSPs**
- Upload as **unlisted and scheduled at T-3** so you have three days of a shareable countdown link
- **Be in the chat.** Reply to everyone. You'll have eleven people there and every one of them will remember it.
- Title: `[ARTIST] — [SONG] (Official Music Video)`
- Pin a comment with `[LINK]` before the premiere starts
- End screen: subscribe + the lyric video (once it exists) + the playlist

### ▸ UPLOAD 2 — OFFICIAL AUDIO / VISUALISER · T

A static image or a slow 6-second loop, with the audio over it, 16:9.

This exists for three reasons and they're all invisible: it's what plays when someone autoplay-chains into you, it's the version people leave running in a background tab (watch time), and it's the canonical upload if the video ever slips. **Twenty minutes of work.** Do not skip it.

Title: `[ARTIST] — [SONG] (Official Audio)`

### ▸ UPLOAD 3 — THE LYRIC VIDEO · T+14

You already have the animation. Its job here is to be the **second content spike** — a fortnight after release, when the first wave has flattened, a second upload gives the algorithm a fresh object and gives you a reason to post about the song again.

Title: `[ARTIST] — [SONG] (Official Lyric Video)`
Description: full lyrics, as text. This is directly searchable and it's how people find a song they half-remember.

### ▸ UPLOAD 4 — DIRECTOR'S COMMENTARY · T+21
**Cost: a microphone and 40 minutes.**

The song plays. You talk over it. Where the line came from, what the chord is doing, the mistake at 1:42 you left in, who it's about and how much of that you'll admit. Visually it's the lyric video or a static — nobody's watching, they're listening.

This is the highest-value low-cost upload on the list. It's 8–12 minutes of watch time from people who already like the song, it deepens the relationship more than any Reel can, and **nobody at your level makes one.**

Title: `I made this song alone in my bedroom — here's every decision, explained`
*(Note the title: it's written for search and curiosity, not for people who already know the song.)*

### ▸ UPLOAD 5 — THE MAKING-OF · T+28 · ⚠️ CONDITIONAL
**Exists only if someone films BTS on a phone at the shoot.**

8–10 minutes, cut from phone footage: set-up, the tenth take, the thing that went wrong, the food, people laughing. Rough is the point — this is the counterweight to the polished video.

If there's no BTS footage, replace this slot with **Upload 8 (extended versions)** moved forward, and accept that the slate is one weaker.

Title: `Making a music video with no budget and three friends`

### ▸ UPLOAD 6 — THE ACOUSTIC ONE-TAKE · T+42
**Cost: one camera, one mic, your desk, one afternoon. This is a capture, not a shoot.**

This is the most important non-MV upload in the plan, because **it isn't only a video — it's a release.** Put it out through DistroKid with its own ISRC and you get a brand-new Release Radar placement, a second editorial pitch, and a genuine event in the back third of the window, for the cost of an afternoon. The density analysis moved this from T+84 to T+42 for exactly that reason.

One take. Don't do twenty. The flaw is the feature.

Title: `[SONG] (Acoustic) — live from my bedroom`

### ▸ UPLOAD 7 — THE RETROSPECTIVE · T+50
**Cost: a phone and ten minutes.**

"Everything I learned releasing my first single as an independent artist from India." Talking head. Real numbers. What worked, what didn't, what it cost, what you'd do again.

This is the single most *shareable* format for an independent artist and it does two jobs: it's evergreen search traffic (people look this up constantly, for years), and it converts a viewer into someone who wants to hear song #2. **It's also the best possible ending to the campaign** — it turns a release into a story with a conclusion.

Title: `I released my first song independently from India. Here's exactly what happened.`

### ▸ UPLOAD 8 — THE EXTENDED VERSIONS · rolling
**Cost: rendering.**

Legitimate, low-effort, real search traffic:
- `[SONG] (Slowed + Reverb)` — genuinely a searched category
- `[SONG] (Sped Up)`
- `[SONG] — 1 hour loop` — absurd, and people genuinely play these
- `[SONG] (Instrumental)`

Two caveats: **release the slowed/sped versions properly if you want them on DSPs** (own ISRCs), and **don't upload more than two or three** — a channel that's half novelty versions of one song reads as spam. One or two, spaced weeks apart.

## 4. SHORTS — THE VOLUME SURFACE

### 4.1 The rule

**Every vertical clip you make goes to Reels, TikTok and Shorts on the same day.** Same file, different title and hashtags. This is four minutes of extra work per day and it is the best-value four minutes in the campaign.

### 4.2 What differs from Reels and TikTok

| | Shorts |
|---|---|
| **Length** | Up to 3 minutes — but **stay at 7–20 seconds** for music clips |
| **Title** | Shorts have a **title field**, and it's a search field. Reels and TikTok don't have this. **Use it properly — this is the single biggest Shorts advantage nobody uses.** |
| **Hashtags** | Put `#Shorts` plus 2–3 real ones in the title |
| **Watermarks** | Demoted. Export clean. |
| **Audience** | Broader and older than TikTok. Less trend-literate, more music-literate. |
| **Lifespan** | **The longest of the three.** Shorts resurface months later routinely. |
| **The funnel** | A Short can link to a related long-form video. **Link every Short to the music video.** This is how Shorts pays for itself. |

## 5. TITLES, THUMBNAILS, DESCRIPTIONS

### 5.1 Titles

Two kinds, and never mix them:

**Canonical music titles** — exact, boring, permanent, because they're how DSPs, Content ID and search resolve your track:
```
[ARTIST] — [SONG] (Official Music Video)
[ARTIST] — [SONG] (Official Audio)
[ARTIST] — [SONG] (Official Lyric Video)
[ARTIST] — [SONG] (Acoustic)
```
Never add "NEW!!", "out now", emoji, or ALL CAPS to these. Metadata discipline is the thing that quietly costs the most money.

**Everything-else titles** — written for a person who has never heard of you:
```
I released my first song independently from India. Here's what happened.
Making a music video with no budget and three friends
I made this song alone in my bedroom — here's every decision, explained
What releasing a song actually costs, itemised
```

### 5.2 Thumbnails

1280×720. Three rules:
- **A face, large, with a clear expression.** Faces out-click everything else and it isn't close.
- **Four words maximum**, in a heavy font, high contrast. Assume it's being viewed at the size of a postage stamp, because it is.
- **One visual identity across the channel** — same font, same two colours, so your videos are recognisable in a sidebar.

For the music video itself, the thumbnail can be a clean frame with the title — music video thumbnails are the one exception to the face rule, because the cover-art look is what people expect.

### 5.3 The description template

Set this as your upload default so you never rewrite it:

```
[SONG] — [ARTIST]
Out now, everywhere: [LINK]

[One paragraph: what the song is, in your voice. Two or three sentences.]

—
LYRICS
[full lyrics — this is directly searchable and it's how people find a half-remembered song]

—
CREDITS
Written, produced and mixed by [ARTIST]
[Video director / DOP / editor / anyone else]
Mastered by [name]

—
[ARTIST]
Instagram: instagram.com/[HANDLE]
TikTok: tiktok.com/@[HANDLE]
X: x.com/[HANDLE]
Spotify: [link]
Contact: [email]

#[ARTIST] #[SONG] #indiemusic
```

**Three things people skip:**
- **The first two lines are all most people see** before "show more". Put the link there.
- **Full lyrics in the description** — free, searchable, high-value.
- **Chapters** (timestamps starting at `0:00`) on anything over 4 minutes — the commentary, the making-of, the retrospective. They create a visible progress bar with labels and measurably improve retention.

### 5.4 Every upload, before you hit publish

- [ ] Custom thumbnail
- [ ] Description with `[LINK]` in the first two lines
- [ ] Full lyrics (music uploads)
- [ ] Chapters (anything over 4 min)
- [ ] Added to 2+ playlists
- [ ] End screen: subscribe + next video + playlist
- [ ] Cards at any point you mention another video
- [ ] Pinned comment with the link and one question to start the thread
- [ ] Tags: `[ARTIST]`, `[SONG]`, genre, comparison artists, `indie india`
- [ ] Language + captions (auto is acceptable on talking heads; **burn captions into the music videos**)
- [ ] "Contains music" / Content ID declarations correct
- [ ] Not marked as "made for kids" (this kills comments and personalisation)

## 7. WHAT TO WATCH

| Metric | Where | Good | If it's bad |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Average view duration** | Analytics → Engagement | >50% on music videos | The first 15 seconds are wrong |
| **Impressions CTR** | Analytics → Reach | 4–8% | The thumbnail or title, nothing else |
| **Traffic source: Browse/Suggested** | Analytics → Reach | Rising over time | If 90% is "External", YouTube isn't recommending you yet — normal early |
| **Shorts → long-form conversion** | Analytics → Shorts | any at all | Add the related-video link to every Short |
| **Returning viewers** | Analytics → Audience | Rising | This is the real health metric |
| **Subscribers per video** | Per upload | — | Ignore total subscriber count for 60 days; watch per-video instead |

Two traps: **do not check analytics daily** in month one — the numbers will be small and it will cost you more in morale than it returns in information. And **do not delete an underperforming video.** It's a permanent asset with a ten-year tail.
