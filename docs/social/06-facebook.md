## 1. THE HONEST THESIS

**Organic reach on a new Facebook Page is effectively zero.** A Page with 40 followers posting a photo reaches four people. Don't build a content strategy around that, don't be disappointed by it, and don't spend an hour a week on it.

But Facebook does three jobs no other platform does, and two of them are genuinely valuable:

| Job | Value | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| **1. It is the ads infrastructure.** You cannot run a single Meta ad without a Page. Your entire $200 ad budget — the highest-leverage spend in the whole plan — depends on this existing and being configured correctly. | **Critical** | 40 minutes, once |
| **2. Groups.** This is the genuinely underrated part and it's where almost all of Facebook's real value is for you. Indian music groups, diaspora community groups, songwriter and producer groups, alumni groups. Groups still have real organic reach where Pages don't. | **High** | 20 min/week |
| **3. Reels crossposting + the older/diaspora audience.** Facebook Reels has meaningfully less creator competition than Instagram's, Meta pushes it hard, and Facebook is where the *older* diaspora actually lives — the aunt in New Jersey who will absolutely stream your song and tell nine people. | **Moderate, free** | 0 min — it's automatic |

**Total weekly time budget for Facebook: 20 minutes.** All of it in Groups. Everything else is automated or one-time setup.

## 3. GROUPS — WHERE THE ACTUAL VALUE IS

Pages are dead. Groups are not. A post in a 20,000-member group with real engagement will out-reach your Page by a factor of a hundred, and it's free.

### 3.1 The rule that makes it work

**Every one of these groups will ban you for a cold self-promotional post, and they'll be right to.** The protocol is the same everywhere:

> **Join at T-45. Comment helpfully on other people's posts for four weeks. Post your own thing once, at T or T+1. Then go back to commenting.**

Four weeks of genuine participation buys you one post. That's the exchange rate and it's non-negotiable. It's also not wasted time — the comments themselves get you seen.

### 3.2 The group taxonomy — find 20–25 across these categories

| Category | What to search | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Indian independent music** | "Indian Indie Music", "Independent Musicians India", "Indian Music Scene" | Your actual peers; some have real curator presence |
| **Indian city scenes** | "[CITY] Musicians", "[CITY] Music Community", "Bands in [CITY]" | Local, small, high engagement, and they show up to things |
| **Diaspora community groups** | "Indians in Toronto", "Desis in the Bay Area", "Indians in Dubai", "Indians in London" | ⭐ **The highest-value category for your diaspora phase.** Huge, active, and they genuinely share things from home. |
| **Alumni / college** | BITS alumni groups, your batch groups, campus society pages | ⭐ The single highest conversion rate of any group on this list. These people know you. |
| **Producer / home-recording** | "Home Recording", "FL Studio Users", "Bedroom Producers", "Mixing & Mastering" | Craft posts do well; these people share things |
| **Songwriter groups** | "Songwriters", "Lyric Writers", "Songwriting Feedback" | Feedback threads are a legitimate, welcome way to share a song |
| **Genre groups** | "[GENRE] Lovers", "Indie Music Discovery", "New Music Friday" | Explicitly exist for discovery |
| **Feedback / share-your-music groups** | "Share Your Music", "Music Feedback Exchange" | Lower quality, but the rules explicitly permit posting. Reciprocity is expected — listen to others' tracks. |

### 3.3 The group post — one template, personalised every time

```
Hi all — I've been reading this group for a couple of months and it's been genuinely useful [name one specific thing you got from it].

I'm an independent artist from [CITY] and I put out my first single on [DATE]. I wrote, produced and mixed it myself. It's [one sentence: what it sounds like, who it's for].

[LINK]

Happy to hear anything honest about it, including the unflattering version. And if anyone here is about to release something, I'll listen to yours.
```

**Why this works:** it establishes you've been present, it's specific, it doesn't oversell, and it offers something back. **Never post a bare link.** Never post the same text in five groups on the same day — Facebook detects that and it's the fastest route to a spam flag.

### 3.4 The weekly 20 minutes

- Comment on **5 posts** across your groups. Answer a question, react to someone's track, say something specific.
- **Listen to one person's song and leave a real comment.** They will listen to yours.
- That's it.

## 4. WHAT TO ACTUALLY POST ON THE PAGE

Low volume. **2 posts a week, plus whatever auto-crossposts from Instagram.** The Page's job is to look alive when someone checks it after seeing an ad, not to generate reach.

### 4.1 The Facebook register

Facebook's audience is **older and less cynical** than every other platform in this plan. That changes the writing:

- **Write in sentences.** Lowercase-fragment Instagram style reads as illegible here.
- **Be more explicit.** "My new song is out — you can listen on Spotify here" works. "out ↓" doesn't; older users won't know what the arrow means.
- **Warmth over irony.** X's dryness lands as rudeness on Facebook.
- **Longer is fine.** Facebook doesn't truncate as aggressively and its readers read.
- **Always say where to listen, by name.** "on Spotify, Apple Music and YouTube" beats "everywhere".

## 5. THE RELEASE EVENT

Create a **Facebook Event** for release day at T-14. It costs ten minutes.

- Name: `[SONG] — out everywhere`
- Date: T, at the release hour
- Cover: the cover art
- Description: the release-day Page post + `[LINK]`
- **Invite your entire friends list.** This feels awkward and you should do it anyway. It's one of the only remaining mechanisms on Facebook that reliably puts a notification in front of people who know you, and people who know you are your first 48 hours.

Post in the Event twice — once at T-1 and once at T.

## 6. FACEBOOK REELS — THE FREE PART

Auto-crossposting from Instagram handles this. Two things worth knowing:

- **Facebook Reels reach is often higher than Instagram Reels reach** for a small account, because the creator competition is thinner and Meta is pushing the surface hard.
- **The audience is different** — older, more diaspora, more tier-2 India. A clip that underperforms on Instagram sometimes does well here.
- **Check Facebook Reels insights once a month.** If something is outperforming there, that tells you something real about who your audience actually is — and it should feed your ad targeting.

No extra work. Just don't turn the crossposting off.
