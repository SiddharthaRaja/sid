## 1. WHAT ACTUALLY DRIVES A FIRST RELEASE

Before any pitching, understand what you're pitching *into*. Spotify doesn't decide whether your song is good. It measures how people behave when they hear it, and it does that mostly in the first 48 hours.

**The four signals that matter, in order:**

| # | Signal | What it is | How to move it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Save rate** | % of listeners who save the track | **Ask for saves explicitly.** Most people don't know it matters. This is the single highest-leverage ask in your entire campaign. |
| 2 | **Completion rate** | % who listen past ~30s and to the end | A tight intro. A song under 3:30. **Tell people not to skip** — a skip in the first 30 seconds is a negative signal. |
| 3 | **Playlist adds** — especially to *personal* playlists | A listener adding it to their own playlist | Ask for this by name. "Add it to any playlist of your own" is the specific phrasing. |
| 4 | **Repeat listens in week one** | Same listener coming back | You can't really force this. It's the song. |

**What these unlock, in order of when:**

- **Release Radar** — goes to your followers automatically, *provided you pitched the track*. Even a rejected pitch guarantees this. **This alone justifies pitching.**
- **Autoplay and Radio** — when someone's queue runs out after a related artist, you can surface. Driven by the behaviour signals above.
- **Discover Weekly** — needs real save and completion data first. Realistically weeks 3–8, if at all.
- **Editorial** — a human decision, informed by all of the above plus your pitch.

**The thing this means practically:** the 40 people you message personally at T-7 are worth more than any playlist submission, because they generate high save rates and high completion in the first 48 hours from an audience Spotify recognises as engaged. **Get the fundamentals right before you spend a rupee on pitching.**

## 2. THE THREE KINDS OF PLAYLIST

People say "playlist pitching" as if it's one activity. It's three, and they have nothing in common.

| | **Editorial** | **Algorithmic** | **Independent / user** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who makes it | Spotify's human editors | Spotify's systems | Anyone |
| Examples | New Music Friday India, Fresh Finds | Release Radar, Discover Weekly, Radio | Someone's "songs for driving at night" |
| How you get on | **One pitch, through Spotify for Artists** | **You don't pitch.** You earn it with save rate and completion. | Email, DM, SubmitHub, Groover |
| Odds for a debut | Low, but not zero — Fresh Finds is genuinely reachable | Moderate, and it compounds | **Moderate to good, and it's where your realistic first wins are** |
| Volume of work | One submission | Zero | 80–120 personalised emails |
| **Where to spend your time** | 2 hours | 0 hours | **20 hours** |

**The honest allocation:** submit the editorial pitch once and forget about it. Spend nothing on algorithmic. Put essentially all of your pitching effort into independent curators — that's where a first release actually gets traction, and it's the only one where effort scales linearly with outcome.

## 3. THE EDITORIAL PITCH

### 3.1 The mechanics, confirmed

| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | Spotify for Artists → Music → Upcoming → **Pitch a Song** |
| **How many** | **One track per release.** Once it's live, it can't be pitched. Choose carefully — pitch the single, not a B-side. |
| **Character limit** | **500** |
| **Minimum lead time** | 7 days |
| **Recommended lead time** | **4–6 weeks** |
| **Fields** | Genre (1) + subgenres (up to 3) · mood · style · instrumentation · culture tags · language · cover? instrumental? explicit? · **promotion plans checkboxes** · the 500-character description |

> ⚠️ **Refinement to the master plan.** It puts the editorial pitch at T-25. Spotify's own guidance and every current pitching guide recommends **4–6 weeks**. **Move your pitch to T-35, which means the DistroKid upload moves to T-38.** This is a free improvement — it costs you nothing but a calendar change, and the pitch has to exist before the editors' weekly meetings for your release week, not during them.

### 3.2 The fields — how to fill them

- **Genre + subgenres:** be *accurate*, not aspirational. Editors filter by these, and a mismatch between your tags and your song is the fastest route to a pass. Pick the subgenre that matches the playlist you actually want.
- **Mood, style, instrumentation, culture tags:** fill in **every single one**. Even if the pitch is rejected, Spotify uses this metadata to place your track algorithmically. A fully tagged track that's rejected editorially still gets better algorithmic placement than an untagged one. **This is the most under-appreciated part of the whole form.**
- **Language:** accurate. If it's English, say English — don't hedge toward Hindi to get into Indian playlists.
- **Promotion plans:** tick everything that's true. Music video ✓ · paid advertising ✓ · playlist campaign ✓ · press campaign ✓. **Editors are picking between hundreds of tracks and choosing the ones that are least likely to flop.** Visible campaign = less risk.

### 3.3 The 500-character description — six variants

Pick one. Count the characters when you fill it in — they're tight, and going over means being truncated mid-sentence.

---

**▸ VARIANT 1 — THE STANDARD** *(the safe, complete version)*
```
"[SONG]" is a [tempo] [GENRE] track built around [the specific hook — what's distinct in the first 15 seconds]. Written, produced and mixed independently in [CITY], India.

For fans of [ARTIST 1], [ARTIST 2], [ARTIST 3]. Fits Fresh Finds India, Indie India, New Music Friday India.

Campaign: self-directed music video day one, 100-day short-form series, paid social across India/US/UK/CA/UAE, 80+ playlist submissions, college radio.

Debut single from a new independent artist.
```

---

**▸ VARIANT 2 — THE STORY** *(leads with what makes you different)*
```
"[SONG]" was written, produced, mixed and released by one person in a bedroom in [CITY] while finishing an engineering degree. It's [GENRE] — [one line on what it sounds like].

The debut from an Indian artist making English-language [GENRE] for a global audience, which is still a rare enough thing that it's the whole pitch.

For fans of [ARTIST 1] and [ARTIST 2]. Music video, 100-day campaign, paid social across five markets. Fits Fresh Finds India.
```

---

**▸ VARIANT 3 — THE SOUND-FIRST** *(for when the track itself is the strongest argument)*
```
"[SONG]" opens on [exact description of the first 10 seconds] and doesn't add [drums/anything] until [timestamp]. [One more concrete production detail.] [X] BPM, [key], [duration].

Self-produced [GENRE] from [CITY], India. For fans of [ARTIST 1], [ARTIST 2], [ARTIST 3].

Sits in Fresh Finds, Indie India, [one more real playlist].

Campaign: music video, 100-day content series, paid social in India/US/UK/CA/UAE, 80+ independent playlist submissions.
```

---

**▸ VARIANT 4 — THE MARKET** *(leads with the diaspora strategy — a genuine differentiator)*
```
"[SONG]" is [GENRE] made in [CITY], India, in English, aimed deliberately at the Indian diaspora in the US, UK, Canada and UAE before the domestic market.

[One line: what the song sounds like and what makes the first 15 seconds distinct.]

For fans of [ARTIST 1], [ARTIST 2]. Fits Fresh Finds India, Indie India.

Campaign: music video, 100 days of short-form, geo-targeted paid social across five markets, 80+ playlist submissions, college radio.
```

---

**▸ VARIANT 5 — THE SHORT ONE** *(~290 characters — confidence reads well)*
```
Self-produced [GENRE] from [CITY], India. "[SONG]" is [one sentence — the hook, the feeling, what's different].

For fans of [ARTIST 1], [ARTIST 2], [ARTIST 3]. Fits Fresh Finds India.

Debut single. Music video day one, 100-day campaign, paid social in five markets.
```

---

**▸ VARIANT 6 — THE ACOUSTIC RE-RELEASE** *(for the T+42 stripped version — a second pitch, a second shot)*
```
"[SONG] (Acoustic)" is one voice, one guitar, one take, recorded in a bedroom in [CITY]. No production.

The original released [DATE] and has [X] streams across [Y] countries with a [Z]% save rate, entirely independently.

For fans of [ARTIST 1], [ARTIST 2]. Fits Fresh Finds India, [acoustic/chill playlist].

Campaign continues: paid social across India/US/UK/CA/UAE, second playlist push.
```

*(⭐ This is why the acoustic version at T+42 matters so much. It's a **new ISRC**, which means a **new editorial pitch** — and this time you have real numbers to put in it. The second pitch is always stronger than the first.)*

### 3.4 What makes a pitch fail

- Adjectives instead of facts. "Beautiful, emotional, unique" tells an editor nothing.
- Naming playlists that don't exist, or naming Today's Top Hits.
- Describing the artist's feelings rather than the track's sound.
- Leaving mood/instrumentation/culture tags blank.
- Pitching 3 days before release.
- Pitching an album track instead of the single.

## 4. FRESH FINDS FORWARD — AIM HERE

This is the most useful thing on this page and it's new enough that most artists don't know about it.

**Fresh Finds** is Spotify's programme for unsigned and independent artists — it's been running for a decade and has driven over 128 million new-artist discoveries since 2024. **Fresh Finds Forward**, which began accepting participants on 1 January 2026, extends it: any artist placed on a Fresh Finds playlist gets automatically invited into a **12-month programme** of resources, guidance and community support. Roughly 10,000 emerging independent artists qualify annually.

**Why this matters to you specifically:**
- Fresh Finds is **explicitly for unsigned, early-career artists.** Unlike almost every other editorial playlist, you are the target demographic rather than an outsider.
- There's a **Fresh Finds India** playlist as well as the global one — two shots.
- It's reachable. Not likely, but reachable — which is more than can be said for most editorial.
- The Forward programme turns one placement into a year of support.

**How to aim at it:** there is no separate application. **You pitch normally through Spotify for Artists, and Fresh Finds is one of the things the editors are picking for.** What you can control:
- Name **Fresh Finds** and **Fresh Finds India** explicitly in your 500-character pitch
- Make sure your "unsigned / self-released / independent" status is unmissable in the first line
- Complete every metadata field
- Pitch at **T-35**, not T-25

## 5. FINDING PLAYLISTS — BUILDING A LIST OF 120 IN AN AFTERNOON

I'm not going to list invented playlist names. Independent playlists appear, die and change hands constantly, and a list of made-up names would waste your time and damage your credibility when you cite one that doesn't exist. **Here is how to generate a real list of 120 live targets, with contactable curators, in about four hours.**

### ▸ ENGINE 1 — "Discovered On" *(the best single method)*
1. Open a comparison artist's Spotify profile → **About** → **Discovered On**
2. That list shows the playlists actually driving their discovery — real playlists with real listeners in your exact lane
3. Do this for **15 artists**: your 3 comparison artists, plus 12 artists at roughly your level in the same lane
4. Expect **8–15 playlists per artist.** That's 120–200 candidates on its own.

⭐ **The refinement that matters:** don't use the huge artists. Use artists with **10k–200k monthly listeners.** A playlist that breaks a 50k-listener artist will consider you. A playlist that features a 5m-listener artist will not.

### ▸ ENGINE 2 — Spotify search by mood and use-case
Curators name playlists after *situations*, not genres. Search these strings directly in Spotify and sort by follower count:

`late night drive` · `sad indie` · `bedroom pop` · `songs to cry to` · `indie chill` · `new indie` · `acoustic mornings` · `rainy day` · `soft indie` · `indie discoveries` · `hidden gems` · `underrated songs` · `indie india` · `desi indie` · `south asian indie` · `english indian artists` · `3am playlist` · `study indie` · `coffee shop indie` · `heartbreak indie` · `lo-fi singer songwriter` · `new music friday indie` · `fresh indie` · `unsigned artists` · `emerging artists` · `first listen`

**The filter:** ignore anything over ~50,000 followers (won't reply) and anything under ~300 (not worth the email). **The sweet spot is 1,000–20,000 followers.**

### ▸ ENGINE 3 — Chartmetric (free tier)
Genuinely useful at zero cost. Search a comparison artist → Playlists tab → every playlist they're on, with follower counts and, often, curator contact details. Faster than doing it by hand.

### ▸ ENGINE 4 — The curator's other playlists
When you find one good playlist, click the curator's profile. Curators who make one good playlist usually make six. **One good find often yields five more.**

### ▸ ENGINE 5 — Directories and paid-feedback platforms
- **SubmitHub** — $1–3 per submission, guaranteed feedback. Buy ~25 credits. Realistic hit rate 5–15%, and the feedback alone is worth the money on a first release.
- **Groover** — ~€2 per submission, guaranteed response, stronger in Europe and includes a radio channel.
- **Daily Playlists**, **Soundplate**, **Indiemono** — free submission directories.
- **MusoSoup** — pay-per-response, mixed quality.

### ▸ ENGINE 6 — Instagram and X curator accounts
A large share of independent curators promote their playlists on Instagram. Search `#spotifyplaylist`, `#playlistcurator`, `#indieplaylist`, `#submityourmusic`. **Many take DM submissions and reply faster than email.**

### ▸ ENGINE 7 — Reddit
`r/SpotifyPlaylistShare`, `r/playlists`, `r/indieheads` (read the rules first — they're strict). Lower yield, zero cost.

### ▸ ENGINE 8 — Your own listeners *(after T+14)*
Spotify for Artists shows which playlists your track has already landed on. **Those curators' other playlists are your warmest possible leads**, and you have a real reason to write: "you added my song to X, thank you — would Y be a fit too?"

## 8. VETTING CURATORS — AND THE PAYOLA PROBLEM

### ⚠️ Never pay for guaranteed placement.

This is not a moral point, it's a practical one. Spotify removes tracks for it, can penalise your artist profile, and bot-driven playlist placements poison your listener data — the algorithm reads thousands of low-completion plays from disengaged accounts as *the song is bad*, and that damage is hard to undo.

**The distinction that makes SubmitHub and Groover legitimate:** you're paying the curator for *their time and a guaranteed response*, not for placement. They can and frequently do say no. Anyone promising "guaranteed adds" or "X thousand streams" is selling you the thing that gets you removed.

### Red flags — skip immediately
- Guarantees placement for money
- Follower count spiked suddenly (check on Chartmetric)
- No social presence, no name, no face
- Generic name: "Top Hits 2026", "Best Songs Ever"
- Stock or AI cover art
- Tracks are stylistically incoherent — 200 songs, no through-line
- Huge follower count, tiny listener count (a dead giveaway of bought followers)
- Asks for money *after* saying yes

### Green flags
- A real person with a real, active social account
- Organic follower growth
- Appears in "Discovered On" for artists at your level
- Stylistically coherent — you can hear the taste
- Updated recently, regularly
- Between 1,000 and 20,000 followers

### 9.3 Follow-up protocol
- **Follow up exactly once**, 10–14 days after the first email, or on release day. Never twice.
- **Never follow up across channels.** If you emailed, don't also DM.
- **A no is information, not a rejection.** Log it and move on.
- **Thank everyone who adds you and share their playlist to your story.** Tag them.

## 10. THE PROFILE — THE SURFACES THAT CONVERT

Getting someone to the track is half. The profile is what turns a listener into a follower, and a follower is what makes release #2 work.

| Surface | Spec | When | Note |

### Things to do on the profile that almost nobody does
- **Make a playlist of your influences** and put it on your profile. It gives a new listener a reason to stay, and it's the kind of thing people screenshot.
- **Follow back the artists on your influences playlist.** Some of them will notice.
- **Update the Artist Pick message every month** with something specific and current — "the acoustic version's out on the 12th" beats "out now" forever.
- **Ask for follows explicitly**, not just saves. "Follow me on Spotify and the next one turns up automatically" is a real, specific, useful thing to say, and Release Radar makes it true.

### Marquee, Showcase and Discovery Mode
- **Marquee / Showcase:** need 5,000+ monthly listeners in the target market. **You're not eligible for this release.** Check at T+7 anyway and note it for release #2.
- **Discovery Mode:** enable only at T+30, and only if there's organic traction worth amplifying. You accept a reduced royalty rate on promoted streams — it's a trade, not free money.

## 11. WHAT NOT TO DO

- ❌ **Never buy streams, followers or guaranteed placements.** It poisons your listener data permanently and can get the track removed.
- ❌ **Never pitch more than one track per release.** You get one.
- ❌ **Never pitch after release.** The window closes at release.
- ❌ **Never send the same email to 80 curators.** They talk to each other, and a generic pitch has a near-zero hit rate anyway.
- ❌ **Never attach an audio file** unless asked.
- ❌ **Never follow up twice.**
- ❌ **Never re-upload the same recording with a new ISRC** to "restart" the campaign. It splits your stream counts and looks like fraud.
- ❌ **Don't obsess over the numbers daily** in month one. Check Monday mornings. That's it.

## 12. THE SHORT VERSION

If you do only six things on Spotify:

1. **Pitch editorially at T-35**, fully tagged, naming Fresh Finds India. Even a rejection guarantees Release Radar.
2. **Build the 120-playlist list using "Discovered On"** for 15 artists at your level.
3. **Send 8–10 personalised emails a day from T-21**, one playlist per email, naming a track on it.
4. **Ask for saves and playlist adds explicitly**, everywhere, in those words.
5. **Upload a Canvas at T-1.**
6. **Thank every curator who adds you, within 48 hours.**
