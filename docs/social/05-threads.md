## 1. THE DIFFERENT EQUATION — YOUR ACTUAL QUESTION, ANSWERED

You said Threads is "kind of an extension of Instagram, so that equation is a little different" and you weren't sure how it should differ from X. Here's the precise version of the thing you were circling:

> **On X, your followers are strangers you earned. On Threads, your followers were handed to you from Instagram.**

Threads accounts are tied to your Instagram account, and Instagram actively pushes your IG followers to follow you on Threads. Which means, roughly from day one:

- **Your Threads audience ≈ your Instagram audience.** The same people.
- **But the Threads algorithm behaves nothing like Instagram's** — it's discovery-heavy, mixing followed accounts with a lot of strangers, and it ranks on *reply likelihood*, *profile-click likelihood*, and *follow likelihood*, not on sends and watch time.

That combination creates one specific trap and one specific opportunity, and everything in this document follows from them.

### ⚠️ The trap
**If you post Instagram material on Threads, the same person sees it twice in ten minutes and unfollows one of you.** Cross-posting your Reels captions to Threads is the single most common mistake here, it's the default behaviour the apps nudge you toward, and it is actively worse than posting nothing.

**If you post your X material on Threads, the tone is wrong.** X rewards dry, flat, slightly cynical. Threads rewards warm, curious, conversational. The exact same sentence underperforms on one and works on the other.

### ⭐ The opportunity
Threads is the **cheapest reach available for text on the internet right now**, and it aggressively shows you to non-followers. The master plan already flags this. A text post on Threads routinely gets 5–20× the reach of the same text on X at the same follower count. And because the ranking signals are reply-likelihood and follow-likelihood, **a post that makes people want to answer is worth more than a post that's good.**

### The resolution, in one line
> **X gets your writing. Instagram gets your images. Threads gets your questions, your process, and your off-cuts — in a warmer voice than X and about things Instagram never sees.**

## 2. THE FOUR APPROACHES — PICK ONE (OR THE BLEND)

You asked for variations. Here are four genuinely different ways to run this account, honestly assessed.

---

### ▸ VARIANT 1 — THE MIRROR
*Cross-post from Instagram and X. Ten minutes a week.*

**What it is:** Turn on IG→Threads sharing, paste X posts, done.

**For:** Costs nothing. The handle stays alive.
**Against:** Your IG followers see everything twice. Your X voice lands flat here. You get the reach of a dead account while spending the goodwill of a live one.

**Verdict: ❌ Don't.** This is the default and it's worse than not having the account. If you genuinely have no capacity, claim the handle, set the bio, post four times across the whole campaign, and leave it. That's better than mirroring.

---

### ▸ VARIANT 2 — THE OPEN NOTEBOOK
*The craft layer. What you're working on, what's hard, what you learned, in real time.*

**What it is:** Threads is where you think out loud about making things. Not the diary (that's X, and it's about a person). Not behind-the-scenes video (that's Instagram). **Text, about the work, while it's happening.** "spent four hours on eleven seconds." "the second verse is broken and i don't know why." "here's the free plugin that fixed a problem i've had for a year."

**For:** It's the most natural fit for Threads' register — warm, specific, unguarded. It's genuinely useful content, which is what gets shared here. It attracts the musician/producer audience, who are the most likely people to follow, reply, and eventually listen. And critically: **it's material no other platform of yours has.**
**Against:** Requires you to actually be working on something and to notice what's interesting about it. Runs thin in the post-release phase when the work is done.

**Verdict: ✅ Strong. The best single-variant answer.**

---

### ▸ VARIANT 3 — THE CONVERSATION ENGINE
*Every post is a question or a prompt. The account exists to generate replies.*

**What it is:** You almost never make statements. "what's a song you love with under 100k streams." "what's the worst advice you've been given about making things." "name a song that's ruined for you and why." Then you reply to every single answer.

**For:** This is the approach most aligned with what Threads actually ranks — reply-likelihood is a primary signal, and a post that gets 40 replies gets shown to thousands of strangers. It's the fastest possible growth curve on this platform. It's also low-effort: a question takes twelve seconds.
**Against:** An account that is *only* questions reads as hollow and slightly manipulative. People notice. It also gives someone no reason to follow you specifically, because you haven't shown them anything of yours.

**Verdict: ✅ Powerful, but not alone.** This is an engine, not a car.

---

### ▸ VARIANT 4 — THE B-SIDES
*Everything that didn't fit anywhere else. Cut lyrics, rejected titles, the take you didn't use, the cover you abandoned, the photo that's slightly wrong.*

**What it is:** Threads as the off-cuts bin. Deliberately minor. "this was going to be the title." "here's the verse i cut." "recorded this cover and it was bad, here it is anyway."

**For:** Infinitely supplied — the off-cuts of one song are enormous. It has a consistent, likeable identity. It's genuinely exclusive content by definition. It's the lowest-pressure posting you'll do anywhere.
**Against:** It's inherently secondary. On its own it never quite becomes a reason to follow.

**Verdict: ✅ Excellent filler, weak spine.**

---

### ⭐ THE RECOMMENDATION: 2 + 3, with 4 as filler

Run **the open notebook as a conversation.** Concretely:

| Share | Type | What |
|---|---|---|
| **50%** | Notebook (V2) | Observations about making the thing, while making it |
| **30%** | Questions (V3) | Real questions you want answers to. **Reply to every answer.** |
| **20%** | B-sides (V4) | The off-cuts, the rejected, the abandoned |

**And roughly 1 in 12 posts is a promo post.** That's it. That ratio is deliberate — Threads punishes promotional density harder than X does, and rewards it far more when it's rare.

This table is the most practical thing in this document. Print it.

| The thought | **X** | **Threads** | **Instagram** |
|---|---|---|---|
| The second verse is hard | `the second verse is where songs go to die` | `does anyone else find the second verse the hardest part? i can write a chorus in an hour and then just sit there` | `the second verse took three weeks 🖤` |
| Release date | `"[SONG]" — [DATE]. three minutes, eight months.` | `ok so my first song comes out on [DATE] and i've been putting off telling anyone because saying it out loud makes it real` | *(the full announcement caption + cover art)* |
| The cost | `here's what a debut single actually pays. posting it because nobody posted it for me.` | `i kept a spreadsheet of everything releasing this song cost. want me to post it? genuinely asking, it's quite bleak` | *(carousel: "what a debut single costs")* |
| Being nervous | `the thing i'm afraid of isn't that people won't like it, it's that nobody hears it at all` | `is it normal to be more scared of no one listening than of people not liking it. asking for me.` | `three days. I keep thinking about the version of me two years ago who couldn't finish anything.` |
| A mistake | `there's a mistake in this song and it's on the record` | `left a mistake on the record on purpose and i keep going back and forth on whether that's artistic integrity or laziness` | *(a Reel: "the mistake I left in")* |

**The pattern:**
- **X** — flat, declarative, ends hard. No question mark.
- **Threads** — warmer, more uncertain, usually opens a door. Often ends in a question or an implied one.
- **Instagram** — more emotional, more polished, has a CTA.

**The Threads tells:** "does anyone else", "genuinely asking", "is it normal to", "want me to", "tell me i'm not the only one". These read as needy on X. They're the native grammar here.

## 5. MECHANICS

### 5.1 The things that are specifically Threads

- **500 characters per post**, for everyone — no paid exception. URLs count in full. Plan around it: the 500-char limit is roughly a solid paragraph.
- **Long text attachments** (up to ~10,000 characters) can be attached separately if you genuinely need length. Use this once or twice — for the "what a release costs" post, say. **Not for the diary.** The diary is X's.
- **Topic tags are not hashtags.** You get essentially one per post, rendered as a pill. Pick the specific one, not the broad one: `#bedroompop` over `#music`.
- **Replies are ranked almost as highly as posts.** A good reply on someone else's popular post can out-reach your own posts by an order of magnitude. This is the single biggest difference from X in practice.
- **Quote posts** work well here and are less aggressive-feeling than on X.
- **Share Threads posts to your Instagram story** — one tap, and it's the one place cross-posting *helps*, because it drives IG followers to a platform where they'll see more of you.

### 5.2 Setup

- [ ] Same `[HANDLE]`
- [ ] Bio — make it explicitly different from your X bio:
  ```
  [GENRE] from [CITY]. making it up as i go and writing it down.
  first single "[SONG]" — [LINK]
  ```
- [ ] **Turn OFF auto-sharing Instagram posts to Threads.** This is the trap in §1. Find it and switch it off before you post anything.
- [ ] Follow ~100 accounts, weighted differently from X: more *makers*, fewer industry people. Threads' music-industry layer is thin; its musician layer is thick.

### 5.3 The reply strategy

This is 50% of what works here.

- **10 replies a day on other people's posts.** Substantive ones.
- **Reply to every single reply on your own posts.** Every one. Threads is small enough that this is possible and it's the entire growth mechanism.
- **When someone answers one of your questions, reply with a follow-up question.** A two-turn exchange is worth ten one-liners.
- **Reply to bigger accounts in your niche.** On Threads a good reply under a 50k-follower post genuinely gets seen by thousands. On X it doesn't. Exploit this.

## 6. CADENCE, GAPS, TIMING

| Phase | Posts/day | Replies/day |
|---|---|---|
| T-45 → T-31 | 2 | 10 |
| T-30 → T-15 | 2 | 10 |
| T-14 → T-1 | 3 | 10 |
| T → T+6 | 4 | 15 |
| T+7 → T+27 | 2 | 15 |
| T+28 → T+55 | 2 | 10 |

**Threads tolerates higher text volume than X.** 2–4 posts a day is normal here and doesn't read as spam.

### Gaps

| Gap between | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Two Threads posts | **90 minutes** (much shorter than X or IG) |
| Two promo posts | **48 hours** — keep the 1-in-12 ratio |
| Posting a thought on Threads and the same thought on X | **never the same thought, in the same words.** Rewrite it in the other register or don't post it. |
| A Threads post and its Instagram equivalent | **the same content should never appear on both**, in any form |

### Times (IST)

| Slot | IST | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Morning** | 9:00–10:00 AM | Good for questions — people answer at the start of their day |
| **Afternoon** | 2:00 PM | Low-competition slot |
| **Prime** | **8:30–9:30 PM** | Best all-round: India evening, UAE evening, UK afternoon, US East late morning |
| Late | 11:30 PM | India late scroll, UK evening |

**Put your questions in the morning slot and your notebook posts in the evening slot.** A question posted at 9pm gets answers overnight when you're asleep and you can't reply, which wastes the whole mechanism.

## 7. WHAT THREADS GETS THAT NOTHING ELSE DOES

| Only on Threads | Never on Threads |
|---|---|
| **The questions.** Your whole conversational layer. | **The diary.** X only. Not even one entry. |
| **The craft notebook** — thinking out loud about the work, in text | **Instagram captions.** Auto-sharing off, permanently. |
| **The B-sides** — cut verses, rejected titles, bad covers | **X's dry voice.** It lands flat here. |
| **The warm, uncertain register** | **Heavy promo.** 1 in 12, maximum. |
| **Replying to strangers as a growth strategy that actually works** | **Photo-led content.** That's Instagram's. |
| **The "what this cost" long-text post** | **Anything you've already posted anywhere else.** |

### The one-sentence separation

> **X: what happened to me. Threads: what I'm figuring out. Instagram: what it looks like.**

If a post could sit comfortably on two of those, it belongs on neither until you've rewritten it.
