## 1. THE SIZE PROBLEM, STATED HONESTLY

| | Bluesky | X |
|---|---|---|
| Registered accounts | ~43 million | — |
| **Monthly active** | **~15 million** | ~550–600 million |
| Daily active | ~4.5 million | — |
| Growth | ~1.6m new signups/month, still growing | Flat to declining |

**Bluesky is roughly 2.5% the size of X by active users.** Your Indian audience there is close to nonexistent. Your Western indie-music audience there is real but small.

The counterweight, and it's genuine: **the engagement quality is much higher.** No ads, a chronological option, a smaller and more deliberate user base, and publishers consistently report several times better per-user conversion than on larger platforms. A follower there is worth more than a follower on X. There are just far fewer of them.

### ⭐ The verdict

> **You were right: make it a mirror of X. Spend ten seconds a day on it. Then spend thirty minutes, once, on the three things in §3 — because those are the only parts of Bluesky that aren't X.**

Do not build a separate content strategy. Do not write original posts for it. If Bluesky ever starts returning more than it costs, you'll notice, and you can escalate then.

## 2. THE MIRROR — WHAT CROSSES OVER AND WHAT DOESN'T

| From X | Mirror to Bluesky? | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Promo posts** (Track A) | ✅ **Yes, all of them** | Straight copy-paste. Change nothing. |
| **The release-day thread** | ✅ Yes | Post it as a thread here too, and pin it |
| **The diary entries** (Track B) | ✅ **Yes** — and this is the interesting one | See below |
| **Chapter threads** | ❌ No | Keep these X-only, so X stays the canonical home of the series |
| **Replies to other people** | ❌ Not mirrored | Reply natively, to Bluesky people, when you're there |
| **Observations / one-liners** | ✅ Yes | Free |

### On mirroring the diary

**Yes, mirror it.** The audiences barely overlap, the marginal cost is ten seconds a day, and the diary is the best writing you'll produce in this campaign — it should be in both places.

Two small refinements that cost nothing:

1. **Post it a few hours after X.** Say 1:00am IST rather than 10:30pm. Keeps X first and means anyone following both doesn't see it twice in a scroll.
2. **Keep the same numbering.** Consistency is what makes the series an object, and it means a Bluesky reader who finds entry 40 can look for the rest.
3. **The chapter threads stay on X.** That's the one thing that makes X the home of the series rather than a co-host.

## 3. THE THREE THINGS THAT AREN'T X

Thirty minutes, once, and these are the entire non-mirror value of the platform.

### ▸ 3.1 Custom feeds — the actual discovery mechanism
Bluesky's closest thing to algorithmic reach is **custom feeds**: user-built feeds that pull in posts matching a rule, which other people subscribe to and read.

- [ ] Find the music, indie-music and new-music feeds. Search the feeds directory for `music`, `indie`, `newmusic`, `musicians`.
- [ ] **Work out what each feed's inclusion rule is** — usually a hashtag or keyword. Many are literally "any post containing #music" or "#nowplaying".
- [ ] **Then include that term in your relevant posts.** That's it. That's the whole mechanism. One word in a post puts you in front of a feed's entire subscriber base.
- [ ] Also look for feeds for **South Asian** and **India** topics — smaller, and you'll be one of very few musicians in them.

This is the single highest-value thing on this page and almost nobody does it.

### ▸ 3.2 Starter packs
Curated lists of accounts that new users can follow in one tap. Being *in* someone's starter pack is the fastest follower growth available on Bluesky.

- [ ] Search for starter packs on `musicians`, `indie music`, `music writers`, `India`
- [ ] Ask politely to be added to relevant ones — this is a normal and accepted request there in a way it wouldn't be on X
- [ ] **Make your own:** "independent artists worth following" — 40 accounts, none of them you except as the creator. Costs ten minutes, and everyone you add gets a notification that you added them. It is the friendliest possible cold outreach and it works.

### ▸ 3.3 The journalists
A meaningful slice of music journalism moved to Bluesky and hasn't moved back — particularly Substack writers, freelance critics, and smaller-publication people. These are exactly the people you're pitching in master plan Category K3.

- [ ] Follow 30 music writers there
- [ ] Reply to them occasionally, usefully, without pitching
- [ ] When you email them at T-21, the name will be familiar. That's the entire play.

## 6. WHEN TO ESCALATE — AND WHEN TO STOP

**Escalate** if, after T+30, any of these are true:
- A post there is outperforming the same post on X
- You're getting replies from music writers
- You've been added to two or more starter packs

Then it's worth writing for it directly.

**Stop** — just leave the account up and post the promo milestones — if by T+30 the diary entries there are getting near-zero engagement. That's not a failure; it's a 15-million-user platform and your audience may simply not be on it. **The handle is still worth holding**, and the custom-feed and journalist plays in §3 keep working whether or not anyone reads the diary.

## 7. WHAT BLUESKY GETS THAT NOTHING ELSE DOES

Honestly? Very little — and that's the correct answer for a platform at this scale.

| Only on Bluesky | Never on Bluesky |
|---|---|
| **Custom feeds** — a genuinely different discovery mechanism | **Original content written just for it.** Not until it earns that. |
| **Starter packs** — the friendliest cold outreach on the internet | **Time.** Two minutes a day, hard cap. |
| **A slice of music journalism that left X** | **Chapter threads.** X keeps those. |
| **A verified domain handle, free** | **Any expectation of streams.** |
